# The temple cousins

lesson: lesson-12

Task 4.3, final band 1 of 3. Seven high-class letters in one lesson, because
they are one idea repeated seven times: each writes a sound the learner
already has a harbour letter for, and moves it to the temple. The old course
spread these seven across lessons 12-15; stated as pairs against ค ช ท พ ฟ ซ ฮ

SEVEN LETTERS CANNOT EACH HAVE A STORY. Lessons 1 to 8 gave every consonant a
narrative with a place, an event and an object left behind, and that does not
survive seven arrivals in one sitting. What lesson 7 bought instead is spent
here: the learner already owns the harbour half of all seven pairs, so each
temple letter costs a shape and an address and nothing else. The stories the
palace holds for these seven (the egg in the alms bowl, the tiger behind the
gate, the chest against the wall) are stated in one line each and left to the
review deck, which is where they are actually drilled.

THE ORDER IS THE SCHEDULING ORDER, and the groups fall out of the gaps in it.
`symbols.ts` priorities: ส 11, ห 13 | ข 20, ผ 22, ถ 23 | ฉ 32, ฝ 33. Three
clusters with real gaps between them, so the lesson arrives in three groups of
two, three and two, commonest first. A learner who stops halfway has the two
that carry the most text.

THE TEMPLE IS EMPTY UNTIL NOW. Lessons 1-11 declare ม น ง ย ว ก ด บ ช ซ พ ฟ ค
ท ฮ ร ล จ ต ป อ — seven mid and fourteen low, and not one high. So the third
district opens today with all seven of its first residents at once, and the
high-live rule lands the same morning. That is the stake the opening slide is
allowed to make, and it is literally true.

ผ/พ AND ฝ/ฟ ARE THE HEAD TURN, AND LESSON 7 ALREADY SAID SO. Its cousin-pairs
slide states the head-direction contrast for both rows. `confusablePairs`
carries the same two as `head-direction`. These are the only two letters here
whose shape cousin and sound cousin are the same letter, so they are the two
cheapest of the seven and are placed accordingly.

ฝ IS PAIRED AGAINST ฟ, NOT AGAINST ผ. The earlier skeleton contrasted the two
new letters with each other, which asks the learner to hold two unlearned
shapes apart. `confusablePairs` pairs each against its already-taught twin.

เ-ียะ HAS NO EXAMPLE WORD and cannot have one — the corpus holds no word with
it. The rule is stated and the absence is stated with it, the way lesson 8
handled the closed form of แ-ะ.

เ-ีย HAS NO CONDITIONAL FORM. It is absent from `conditionalVowelForms`, so
