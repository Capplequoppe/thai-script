## exposition seven-at-once
heading: Seven letters, one move
- Every letter on this page writes a sound you can already read. What changes is the district: each one takes a sound the harbor owns and gives it to the temple.
- ข is temple kh — cousin of harbor ค. ฉ is temple ch — cousin of ช. ถ is temple th — cousin of ท. ผ is temple ph — cousin of พ. ฝ is temple f — cousin of ฟ. ส is temple s — cousin of ซ. ห is temple h — cousin of ฮ.
- Nothing new arrives at your ear. Everything new arrives at the tone, because tone is decided by district.

## exposition why-the-doubles-exist
heading: Why Thai writes one sound twice
- A harbor letter starting a syllable can never make that syllable rise. A temple letter does it as its default. The doubling is not waste — it is how the script reaches tones one district alone could not.
- So a word spelled with ข and a word spelled with ค can differ by tone alone, and the letter choice is what tells you which you are hearing.

## rule high-live-rule
rule: high-live

## exposition rising-everywhere
heading: The temple's home tone, in real words
- A temple letter opening a live syllable gives a rising tone, and the commonest words in the language demonstrate it over and over.
- ฉัน (chǎn) is I. ผม (phǒm) is I for a man. ของ (khǎawng) is of, or a thing. ถึง (thǔeng) is to reach. หา (hǎa) is to look for. ขอ (khǎaw) is to ask for.
- Say any two of them in a row and you can feel the shape: each one climbs.

## exposition telling-cousins-apart
heading: Keeping each pair apart on the page
- ข and ค share a body; the temple one ข opens its loop inward at the top, the harbor one ค turns it outward. เขา (khǎo) — he or she — climbs because the temple one is doing the writing.
- ถ sits flat like ท but coils its entry. ถาม (thǎam) is to ask; เรียน (riian) is to study — harbor ร opens it, so it stays level while ถาม rises.
- ผ and ฝ are the bee and the lid: ฝ raises a taller stem than ผ, the way a lid stands above what it covers. ฝัน (fǎn) is to dream.
- ส coils like ซ but lives uphill; สูง (sǔung) — tall — is its own reminder. เสีย (sǐa), to lose or to break, opens with it too.

## exposition ia-vowel
heading: เ-ีย — the ia glide
- เ-ีย wraps three signs around its consonant: เ in front, ี above, ย after. Read them as one long vowel, ia.
- เสีย (sǐa) is that vowel under a temple s — live syllable, so it rises. เรียน (riian) is the same vowel under harbor ร — live again, but the harbor keeps it level.
- A short mate, เ-ียะ, exists and is rare; clip the glide short and stop it.

## retrieval which-cousin
reveal: which-cousin-answer
prompt: เขา (khǎo, he or she) rises. Respell it with harbor ค in place of ข and the climb disappears. Same kh sound, same vowel — what is the one written difference doing all of that work?

## reveal which-cousin-answer
retrieval: which-cousin
- The district. เขา is spelled with ข, the temple kh, and a temple letter on a live syllable gives rising tone. ค writes the identical sound from the harbor, where a live syllable stays mid.

## retrieval which-letter
reveal: which-letter-answer
prompt: You hear thǎam — to ask — rising. Two letters write th: ถ and ท. Which one must be on the page, and why?

## reveal which-letter-answer
retrieval: which-letter
- ถ, the temple th. A rising tone on a live syllable is the temple's signature; harbor ท cannot produce it without help.

## exposition close
heading: What you can now read
- All seven temple cousins: ข ฉ ถ ผ ฝ ส ห. Every kh, ch, th, ph, f, s and h sound in Thai now has both of its spellings in your hands.
- One rule carried the whole lesson: temple letter, live syllable, rising tone.
