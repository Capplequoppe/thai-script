# The boat and the monkey, twin masts, and two letters that land as n

## exposition raaw-ruuea
heading: raaw ruuea, a boat flying its flag
- ร sits with its head low at the waterline and a pennant line flying up and hooking over to the right — a boat's prow with a flag on it.
- Harbor, so low class. At the front of a syllable it is a rolled r, the outboard motor turning over: รัก (rák) is love, รับ (ráp) is to receive.
- Thai speakers roll it hard in careful speech and barely at all in fast speech. Both are normal. What matters for reading is that it is there.

## exposition laaw-ling
heading: laaw ling, a monkey on a post
- ล has its head at the base and one long tail arching right over its back — a monkey crouched on a harbor post.
- Harbor again, low class, and the sound is l lapped off the tongue ridge. ลูก (lûuk) is a child or a piece of fruit. ลม (lom) is wind.
- ล and ร look nothing alike at the front of a word and behave identically at the end of one, which is the next slide.

## exposition landing-as-n
heading: Both of them land as n
- At the tail of a syllable neither letter keeps its own sound. Both flatten into n.
- การ (gaan) is the commonest word built this way: written with ร in last position, pronounced with n. It turns up inside longer words unchanged — รายการ (raai-gaan), a programme or a list, and ทางการ (thaang gaan), official.
- This is not an exception you memorise per word. It is what ร and ล always do in final position, and it is why lesson two's live-endings rule names five letters without naming either of these: they are not a new ending, they are น arriving in disguise.

## retrieval final-r
reveal: final-r-answer
prompt: A syllable is written with ร in last position. What sound ends it, and is the syllable live or dead?

## reveal final-r-answer
retrieval: final-r
- It ends in n.
- Live. น is one of the five endings that keep a syllable live, and ร landing as น inherits that — so การ is a live syllable and takes the live tone rule for its class.

## exposition ae-vowels
heading: แ and แ-ะ, two masts instead of one
- แ is the mast of lesson seven raised twice, side by side, and written in front of its consonant in the same way. The sound is a long flat ae, the vowel in the English word cat, stretched.
- แดง (daaeng) is red. แพง (phaaeng) is expensive. แทน (thaaen) is instead of.
- แ-ะ is the short one: twin masts with the stacked hooks after. It behaves exactly like เ-ะ did last lesson — add a final consonant and the hooks become the ็ roof.
- แยก (yâaek) is to separate or a junction, and it is worth saying out loud beside แรก (râaek), first. One vowel, two words, no overlap in meaning at all.

## retrieval one-mast-two
reveal: one-mast-two-answer
prompt: You know one mast and two masts. Say which sound each carries, and what both of them do to reading order.

## reveal one-mast-two-answer
retrieval: one-mast-two
- One mast, เ, is ee. Two masts, แ, is ae.
- Each one is put down ahead of its consonant and voiced after it. The eye takes them first; the mouth takes them second.

## exposition more-words
heading: Words you can read now
- และ (láe) is and, ละ (lá) is each, and ระดับ (rá dàp) is a level or grade.
- ราคา (raa-khaa) is a price. ดูแล (duu laae) is to look after. ทะเล (thá lee) is the sea.
- เล็ก (lék) is small and เร็ว (reo) is fast — both wearing the ็ roof from last lesson.
- ลึก (lúek) is deep, รีบ (rîip) is to hurry, and รัก (rák) you already have. That is four of the twenty commonest verbs in the language, all readable with what you now hold.
