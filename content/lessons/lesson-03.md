# Three stops with no breath, long ii, and where a syllable dies

## exposition three-plain
heading: Three letters that stop the air and release nothing
- gaaw gài, daaw dèk and baaw bai-mái are stops: the air halts completely, and not one of them pushes out a puff of breath behind it.
- Put a palm up close to your lips. English "key" and "pin" blow air onto it. ก, ด and บ should not move it at all.
- That missing puff is worth noticing now. It is the whole reason these three behave as a group, and a later lesson turns it into a shortcut.

## exposition plain-shapes
heading: Reading the three shapes
- ก has no circle anywhere on it — an open frame with a single beak-line arching to the left.
- ด is a bowl with a smooth rim, its head curled inside, drawn down to a point at the bottom.
- บ is the same bowl with both walls stopping level at the rim, open like a basket waiting to be filled.

## retrieval no-puff
reveal: no-puff-answer
prompt: Say gaaw, daaw and baaw with a palm held close to your lips. What should you feel, and what would feeling something tell you?

## reveal no-puff-answer
retrieval: no-puff
- Nothing should move. These three release no breath at all.
- If your hand moves, you are saying the English sound instead — and Thai keeps the breathy versions as separate letters, so the difference changes the word.

## exposition long-ii
heading: The long vowel ี
- ี rides above its consonant like a beret, the brim flicking up at the right edge.
- Hold it: it is the ee of "green", strung out through a smile rather than clipped.
- มี (mii) is to have, or there is. ดี (dii) is good. Two of the commonest words in the language, and you can now read both.

## exposition stops-at-the-end
heading: Where a syllable stops dead
- Last lesson five letters closed a syllable and left it ringing. These three do the opposite: they cut it off.
- มาก (mâak) is much or very, and ยาก (yâak) is difficult. Both end on the k-stop of ก, sealed at the back of the throat with nothing released.
- นก (nók) is a bird — short, stopped, and finished almost before it starts. บวก (bùak) is to add.

## exposition mid-live-practice
heading: Mid class, live syllable
- ก, ด and บ are the first mid class letters you have met, and a live syllable one of them opens lands flat — the same place a low class live syllable lands.
- ดี (dii) is good. กา (gaa) is a crow. บน (bon) is on top of something. บาง (baang) is some, or thin.
- Two classes, one tone, for now. The classes pull apart the moment a syllable stops dead, which is the next rule you get.

## retrieval which-is-dead
reveal: which-is-dead-answer
prompt: One of มี and มาก ends live and the other ends dead. Which is which, and what exactly makes the difference?

## reveal which-is-dead-answer
retrieval: which-is-dead
- มี is live: it ends on a long vowel with nothing after it, so the sound can run on.
- มาก is dead: ก seals the syllable at the back of the throat and the sound has nowhere to go.
- The initial letter is the same in both. Only the ending decides.
