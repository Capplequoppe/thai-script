## exposition aaw-aang
heading: aaw àang, an empty basin
- อ is a plain ring standing open — a basin set down among the market stalls, and the market is the class: อ is mid.
- You have already been reading it. Since lesson six it has been the prop under a bare ื, in มือ and คือ, and nothing was asked of it there.
- It has two jobs, and which one it is doing depends only on where it sits in the syllable.

## rule o-ang-dual-role
rule: o-ang-dual-role

## exposition job-one
heading: Job one — minding the stall
- Thai syllables are written consonant first. A word whose first sound is a vowel has no consonant to write, so อ stands in the slot and says nothing.
- อีก (ìik) is more or again. อัน (an) is the classifier for small things. อา (aa) is a parent's younger sibling. อาจ (àat) is may or might.
- In every one of those the ring is silent. It is holding the place, exactly as it held the place under ื.

## exposition job-two
heading: Job two — being the vowel aaw
- Put อ after a consonant with no vowel written, and it stops being silent and becomes a long aaw — a yawn with the jaw dropped all the way.
- พอ (phaaw) is enough. รอ (raaw) is to wait. ตอน (dtaawn) is a part or a moment. นอน (naawn) is to sleep. มอง (maawng) is to look at.
- Two jobs, one glyph, and the position settles it every time: in front of the vowel slot it is a silent consonant, in the vowel slot it is aaw.

## retrieval which-job
reveal: which-job-answer
prompt: Two syllables, each written with อ. In one the ring is the first character; in the other it follows a consonant with nothing between. Which is silent, and which is a vowel?

## reveal which-job-answer
retrieval: which-job
- First character: silent. It is minding an empty consonant slot for a word that opens on a vowel.
- After a consonant with nothing between: the vowel aaw.
- You never have to guess. The position is the rule.

## exposition short-aw
heading: เ-าะ, the clipped yawn
- เ-าะ is the short version of the same sound: the mast in front, then the า post and the stacked hooks — three pieces wrapping one very short syllable.
- เกาะ (gàw) is an island, and it is dead-short: the vowel is clipped and nothing follows it.
- Beside it, ออก (àawk) is long. Same aw, held, and then stopped by ก. The two lengths are different vowels in Thai, not a matter of emphasis.

## exposition the-grid-closes
heading: The market's four corners
- Mid class live you have had since lesson three: ตาม (dtaam) sits level.
- Mid class dead is one tone whichever length the vowel is, and that tone is low. เกาะ (gàw) is dead-short and low. ออก (àawk) is dead-long and low. จาก (jàak), ปาก (bpàak) and ตก (dtòk) are all the same corner.
- That closes the market's tone grid. Every syllable opening on ก ด บ จ ต ป or อ now has a tone you can work out from the page alone, live or dead, long or short.
- Compare the low class, which needed two dead rules because the lengths disagree — short goes high, long falls. The market is the simpler district in every direction.

## retrieval close-the-grid
reveal: close-the-grid-answer
prompt: บอก and เกาะ both open on a market letter and both end dead, one long and one short. What tone does each take, and how many rules did you need?

## reveal close-the-grid-answer
retrieval: close-the-grid
- Both are low.
- One rule, effectively. A market letter on a dead syllable gives low, and vowel length makes no difference to it — which is exactly what the harbor does not do.

## exposition more-words
heading: Words you can read now
- ชอบ (châawp) is to like, บอก (bàawk) is to tell, ตอบ (dtàawp) is to answer, and ยอม (yaawm) is to consent.
- ออก (àawk) is to go out, and you will see it on every exit sign in the country.
- อาชีพ (aa chîip) is an occupation. อายุ (aa yú) is age — the word Thai uses where English says how old someone is.
- Eleven lessons in: seven of the nine market letters, the whole harbor side of the seven pairs, and twenty vowel spellings.
