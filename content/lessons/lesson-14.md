## exposition the-long-tail
heading: Ten letters you will mostly wave at
- The alphabet has a long tail: ten letters that together carry less than one word in a hundred. You need to recognise them; you will rarely write them.
- They are ฐ ฎ ฏ ฑ ฒ (mostly inside borrowed spellings), ฬ ฆ ฌ (rare doubles of ล ค ช), and ฃ ฅ — two letters modern Thai has retired outright.
- The review queue schedules these at the lowest priority. They stay in the deck forever, but they will never crowd out the letters that carry the language.

## exposition where-they-hide
heading: Where the tail actually shows up
- ฐ, temple th, ends รัฐ (rát) — state — and opens ฐาน (thǎan) — base.
- ฏ, market dt, sits inside ปฏิบัติ (bpà-dtì-bàt) — to carry out. ฎ is its market d twin, and both wear a crown-like base.
- ฑ appears in บัณฑิต (ban-dìt) — a graduate. ฒ in พัฒนา (phát-thá-naa) — to develop. ฬ is the harbor l of กีฬา (gii-laa) — sport. ฆ opens โฆษณา (khôot-sà-naa) — advertising — and ends เมฆ (mêek) — cloud.
- ฌ is the rarest consonant in the alphabet; outside a handful of ceremonial words you will meet it on charts and nowhere else.

## exposition the-retired-two
heading: ฃ and ฅ — retired, still counted
- ฃ (temple kh) and ฅ (harbor kh) sit in every alphabet chart and in no modern word. Typewriters dropped them a century ago and the spellings that used them moved to ข and ค.
- They are still letters of the alphabet, so they are still in your deck — at the very back of the queue.

## exposition ua-uea
heading: The last two vowel families
- -ัว is ua: ตัว (dtua) — body, or the classifier for animals — and หัว (hǔua) — head, rising on its temple ห.
- เ-ือ is uea, three signs around one consonant: เสือ (sǔuea) is tiger, เรือ (ruuea) is boat, เดือน (duuean) is month, เมือง (muueang) is city or town.
- Each has a clipped short mate, -ัวะ and เ-ือะ, both rare on the page.

## exposition rare-vowel-signs
heading: Four vowel signs that act like syllables
- ฤ writes r plus a short vowel in one sign: ฤดู (rúe-duu) is season, and อังกฤษ (ang-grìt) — English — hides it mid-word.
- Lengthen it with ๅ and you get the long form; ฦ and its long form are its l-shaped mates, kept for completeness and effectively extinct.
- Treat all four as spellings to recognise, not machinery to learn rules for.

## retrieval spot-the-rare
reveal: spot-the-rare-answer
prompt: เมฆ (cloud) is read mêek and falls. Its last letter is not the k you usually see. Which letter is it, and what does it tell you about the word?

## reveal spot-the-rare-answer
retrieval: spot-the-rare
- ฆ, the rare harbor kh. As a final it just stops the syllable like any k — its real message is that เมฆ is a borrowed, bookish spelling, which is exactly where the tail letters live.

## retrieval crown-twins
reveal: crown-twins-answer
prompt: ฎ and ฏ are the crown-wearing twins of ด and ต. ปฏิบัติ uses one of them — which sound does the ฏ carry, and which district does it sit in?

## reveal crown-twins-answer
retrieval: crown-twins
- ฏ is dt, exactly as ต is, and it sits in the market. The twins change no sound and no class — they only mark the word as an old borrowing.

## exposition close
heading: The alphabet is complete
- All forty-four consonants are now yours, every written vowel family, and the four rare signs. Nothing on a Thai page is an unknown shape any more.
- What remains for the course is machinery, not letters: the vowels that are not written, the tone marks, clusters, and the leading consonants.
